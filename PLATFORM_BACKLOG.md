# Smart Kasua Platform Backlog

This is a separate, unchecked implementation backlog. The current React app is a browser-local demo; none of the backend order guarantees, AI services, or administrator controls below are implemented. Check an item only after its behavior, permissions, persistence, and failure paths are implemented and tested. AI operating costs are relative estimates until a provider, region, model, and traffic level are chosen.

## 1. Multi-Merchant Cart and Order Management

### Data model and invariants

- [ ] Create a `master_orders` record per customer checkout, with customer, delivery-address snapshot, currency, customer consent for partial fulfilment, totals, and overall lifecycle state.
- [ ] Create one `merchant_suborder` for each distinct merchant in a master order; each has a required `master_order_id`, merchant, merchant-specific items/totals, fulfillment state, and acceptance deadline.
- [ ] Create immutable order-line snapshots for product/offer identity, merchant, description/specification, unit price, quantity requested, quantity accepted, quantity shipped, tax/fee allocation, and cancellation/refund quantities. Never recalculate historical lines from a subsequently edited listing.
- [ ] Create one or more shipment records per merchant suborder; link shipment line quantities, hub handoffs, carrier/rider, tracking events, delivery attempts, and proof of delivery.
- [ ] Create payment records for provider, idempotency key, provider transaction/event IDs, amount, currency, allocation to master/suborders, state, verification timestamps, and reconciliation state. Store no card credentials.
- [ ] Create append-only cancellation and refund records linked to the requesting customer, affected lines/suborders, amount, reason, decision, provider refund, and timestamps.
- [ ] Create a transactional outbox for customer/merchant notifications and an append-only order event history with actor, event type, aggregate/record IDs, timestamp, reason, and correlation ID.
- [ ] Enforce referential integrity and amount invariants: master totals reconcile to line values, fees/discounts and suborder allocations; refunds cannot exceed captured, unrefunded allocations; shipped/delivered quantities cannot exceed accepted quantities.
- [ ] Support partial fulfilment: a master order remains open while eligible suborders/shipments continue; each suborder and line can progress, fail, cancel, or refund independently.

### Required state transitions

- [ ] Model master, suborder, payment, shipment, refund, and complaint states as separate validated state machines. Reject illegal transitions server-side; keep every accepted transition in event history.
- [ ] Use a database transaction and inventory reservation/locking to validate offers, reserve all requested quantities, create the master order, suborders, lines, and a pending payment attempt atomically. Expire reservations safely if payment does not complete.
- [ ] Require explicit customer choice for partial-order acceptance or substitutions. Do not silently replace, remove, or charge for unavailable items.
- [ ] Initiate and verify payment on the server. Treat the browser redirect as informational, not proof of payment. Verify webhook signatures and reconcile provider state before marking an order paid.
- [ ] Make checkout and provider callbacks idempotent using client idempotency keys, unique provider event/transaction constraints, and duplicate-safe state transitions. Retry transient failures with bounded backoff; quarantine poison events and provide manual reconciliation.
- [ ] Use a transactional outbox for notifications so a committed order transition cannot lose its notification; deduplicate notification sends by event/channel/recipient.
- [ ] Add reconciliation jobs for stale payment attempts, reservations, refunds, shipments, and undelivered outbox events. Alert staff when automatic reconciliation cannot resolve a discrepancy.

### Failure and exception policies

- [ ] **Out of stock:** reserve stock atomically before payment. Return unavailable/changed lines and current stock to checkout; require customer confirmation to remove or adjust them. Do not capture payment for the rejected quantities.
- [ ] **Merchant rejection/timeout:** cancel only the affected suborder. Continue other suborders only if the customer allowed partial fulfilment; refund or release that suborder's allocated amount and notify the customer with the reason and revised total/status.
- [ ] **Payment failure:** do not mark paid or release fulfilment work. Keep a retryable payment-attempt record, expire stock holds after a configured deadline, and let the customer resume or abandon checkout without creating a second master order.
- [ ] **Duplicate payment notification:** persist provider event IDs under a unique constraint; acknowledge already-processed events without repeating capture, order transition, refund, or notification side effects.
- [ ] **Partial cancellation:** permit cancellation only for unfulfilled quantities under the published policy; calculate the affected line/suborder allocation, release reserved stock, record the decision, and refund only the eligible paid amount.
- [ ] **Partial refund:** record a line- and fee-level refund allocation against the original captured payment; prevent over-refund, retry provider calls idempotently, and do not represent a requested refund as paid until provider confirmation.
- [ ] **Delayed goods:** compare promised and actual milestones; notify customer and merchant, record the reason, offer continue/cancel options for affected lines, and escalate missed service-level thresholds.
- [ ] **Split shipments:** allow several shipment records per suborder and partial line quantities per shipment; provide independent tracking and keep the master order incomplete until all accepted quantities resolve.
- [ ] **Failed delivery:** record attempt time, outcome, evidence, and rider; notify the customer and offer an allowed retry, collection, or return-to-hub path. Never mark delivery complete without the configured proof.
- [ ] **Customer complaint:** open a case linked to master order, suborder, line, shipment, and payment as applicable; preserve evidence, set response deadlines, and route safety/fraud/financial cases to trained staff.
- [ ] **Merchant dispute:** preserve both parties' evidence and event history, apply the published response/appeal window, keep disputed funds under an explicit policy, and require an authorized reviewer to resolve the case.
- [ ] **Network interruption/repeated requests:** client retries reuse the same idempotency key; server transactions and unique constraints prevent duplicate orders/charges; return a queryable checkout status after reconnect. Never rely on local storage for financial truth.

### Acceptance tests

- [ ] A cart containing five items from four merchants yields one master, four suborders, five lines, and correctly reconciled payment allocations.
- [ ] A merchant rejection refunds only its eligible allocation while other opted-in suborders continue.
- [ ] Concurrent buyers cannot reserve the same final unit; a stale payment releases its reservation exactly once.
- [ ] Replaying checkout, payment webhooks, refund webhooks, and notification jobs produces no duplicate financial or customer-visible effects.
- [ ] Partial quantities can ship, cancel, refund, and complain about independently while master/suborder totals and status history remain reconcilable.

## 2. Modular AI Intelligence Layer

### Shared implementation and governance requirements

- [ ] Define versioned service contracts so each capability can be enabled, disabled, replaced, or run in shadow mode independently of checkout and order correctness.
- [ ] Use deterministic search, database aggregation, policy rules, or conventional optimization when adequate; add a learned model only after a measured gap and sufficient lawful data exist.
- [ ] Apply data minimization, tenant/role authorization, encryption in transit/at rest, retention limits, provider no-training terms where available, and redaction of direct identifiers before external model calls.
- [ ] Log model/rules version, input-data references, decision/output, latency, cost, fallback, and human override without copying unnecessary sensitive input into logs.
- [ ] Set per-feature budgets, rate limits, caching rules, latency limits, offline evaluation, drift/quality monitoring, and a kill switch. Never let generated output change money movement, verification approval, refunds, or safety decisions without authorized human confirmation.
- [ ] Treat outputs as suggestions with citations/provenance where applicable. Protect retrieval and tools from prompt injection and restrict AI tools to least-privilege, read-only operations by default.

Cost labels: **Near-zero** = conventional queries/rules; **Low** = small on-demand model or embedding use with limits; **Medium** = repeated inference, vector indexing, or forecast workloads; **Variable** = depends heavily on volume/provider. These are relative planning estimates, not vendor quotes.

### A. Customer Intelligence

- [ ] **C1 Natural-language product search.** Problem: map local phrasing, misspellings, and intent to relevant offers. Data: catalog names, categories, attributes, aliases, location, stock; launch availability: partial seeded names/categories only. Method: deterministic normalization, synonyms, and filters first; evaluate embeddings/reranking later. Benefit: better recall. Limits: sparse catalog/local-language ambiguity. Cost: near-zero first, low/medium later. Metrics: NDCG@k, recall@k, zero-result rate, latency. Privacy/security: avoid personal profile data in query logs; rate-limit. Oversight: review quality samples and unsafe/biased results. Fallback: existing keyword/category/price search.
- [ ] **C2 Product comparisons.** Problem: help compare materially equivalent offers. Data: normalized specifications, size, condition, authenticity evidence, price, fees, delivery, returns; launch availability: no structured specs yet. Method: deterministic comparison templates with strict product-identity matching; no LLM needed for calculations. Benefit: understandable trade-offs. Limits: missing/incomparable attributes; never merge materially different items. Cost: near-zero. Metrics: attribute completeness, comparison correction rate, task completion. Privacy/security: public offer fields only. Oversight: catalog staff resolve identity ambiguity. Fallback: show separate listings and unknown fields.
- [ ] **C3 Personalized recommendations.** Problem: surface relevant products to an opted-in customer. Data: consented browsing/cart/purchases, explicit preferences, offer availability; launch availability: no trustworthy history. Method: popular-within-category/location and rule-based recommendations first; collaborative/hybrid model only after volume. Benefit: discovery. Limits: cold start, bias, feedback loops. Cost: near-zero first, medium later. Metrics: relevant click/add rate, conversion, diversity, complaint/opt-out rate. Privacy/security: explicit opt-in, purpose limitation, deletion controls. Oversight: monitor exposure and protected-group impacts. Fallback: category bestsellers or curated offers.
- [ ] **C4 Alternative product suggestions.** Problem: find acceptable substitutes for unavailable/over-budget items. Data: structured attributes, variants, price, stock, condition, delivery and customer constraints; launch availability: partial. Method: deterministic hard constraints and weighted similarity; AI only for query interpretation. Benefit: useful substitutes without unsafe equivalence claims. Limits: substitutes are not identical; medical/safety-sensitive items need stricter rules. Cost: near-zero. Metrics: acceptance rate, return rate, mismatch complaints. Privacy/security: use only the current search/cart context. Oversight: customer chooses; no automatic substitution. Fallback: show other available offers and state differences.
- [ ] **C5 Conversational shopping assistance.** Problem: answer product-discovery questions in natural language. Data: current catalog/offers, delivery/return policies, optionally customer-approved cart; launch availability: policy/catalog data incomplete. Method: retrieval-grounded LLM with read-only tools and source citations. Benefit: easier discovery. Limits: hallucinations, stale stock, ambiguous advice. Cost: low/variable per session; cap tokens and requests. Metrics: grounded-answer rate, successful searches, escalation rate, latency/cost. Privacy/security: redact PII, no payment/identity data in prompts, prompt-injection controls. Oversight: sample review and escalation rules. Fallback: ordinary search and support contact.

### B. Merchant Intelligence

Oversight: allow correction/report issue. Fallback: raw verified counts.
- [ ] **M2 Demand insights.** Problem: estimate likely demand by item/location/time. Data: timestamped sales, stockouts, seasonality, market calendars; launch availability: insufficient. Method: seasonal-naive baselines first, then evaluated time-series forecasts. Benefit: planning. Limits: short/noisy rural-market histories, price shocks. Cost: low/medium batch compute. Metrics: WAPE/MAE, stockout rate, overstock rate. Privacy/security: aggregate merchant data; disclose peer-data use. Oversight: show uncertainty and let merchant decide. Fallback: historical averages and manual judgment.
- [ ] **M3 Listing improvement suggestions.** Problem: identify missing or unclear listing data. Data: listing schema, category requirements, quality policy; launch availability: basic names/category/unit/price/stock only. Method: deterministic completeness and validation rules; optional LLM wording suggestions. Benefit: more complete, searchable listings. Limits: generated wording may invent facts. Cost: near-zero rules, low optional model. Metrics: completeness, rejection/misdescription rate. Privacy/security: do not expose identity documents or private sales. Oversight: merchant approves every edit. Fallback: field-level validation errors.
- [ ] **M4 Pricing comparisons.** Problem: inform merchant about comparable local price ranges. Data: matched product identity/specification, location, time, condition, offer price/fees; launch availability: catalog matching not ready. Method: SQL robust ranges after verified matching; no generative price setting. Benefit: informed pricing. Limits: stale/unmatched/outlier offers; do not imply regulated/mandated prices. Cost: near-zero query. Metrics: match precision, freshness, price-check coverage. Privacy/security: anonymize competitors, no confidential transaction prices. Oversight: show source/time and merchant chooses price. Fallback: say insufficient comparable data.
- [ ] **M5 Restocking recommendations.** Problem: indicate when stock may run out. Data: on-hand/reserved stock, lead times, sales velocity, seasonality; launch availability: seeded stock only. Method: deterministic reorder point/safety stock first; forecast only with sufficient history. Benefit: fewer avoidable stockouts. Limits: inaccurate stock/lead times. Cost: near-zero first, low batch compute. Metrics: stockout rate, stale stock, recommendation acceptance. Privacy/security: merchant-only inventory. Oversight: merchant confirms orders. Fallback: threshold alerts.
- [ ] **M6 Customer question summaries.** Problem: condense repeated product/order questions for a merchant. Data: consented, authorized messages linked to listings/orders; launch availability: messaging absent. Method: LLM summarization with source-message links and redaction. Benefit: faster responses. Limits: omit nuance or misstate urgency. Cost: low/variable by message volume. Metrics: summary correction rate, response time, missed-issue rate. Privacy/security: strict tenant isolation and retention; exclude payment credentials. Oversight: merchant reviews before acting. Fallback: chronological message list.

- [ ] **X3 Sales concentration monitoring.** Problem: spot excessive order/revenue concentration. Data: order lines, merchant categories, geography, exposure; launch availability: demo-only orders. Method: deterministic concentration indices and thresholds. Benefit: early fairness/policy visibility. Limits: concentration may reflect real supply/quality. Cost: near-zero batch queries. Metrics: coverage, alert precision, concentration trend. Privacy/security: reports restricted to staff; aggregate where possible. Oversight: policy review before action. Fallback: scheduled static report.
- [ ] **X4 Merchant visibility monitoring.** Problem: measure impressions, positions, clicks, and exposure by eligible merchant. - [ ] **X5 Product category trend analysis.** Problem: identify changing product demand. Data: time-stamped searches/orders/stockouts by category/location; launch availability: insufficient. - [ ] **X6 Fraud-risk alerts.** Problem: prioritize suspicious account/order/payment patterns. Data: verified payment events, device/network signals where lawful, account/order events, reviewed fraud labels; launch availability: no verified labels. Method: auditable rules first; calibrated supervised model only after representative labels. Benefit: triage suspicious events. Limits: false positives and adversarial adaptation; never auto-confiscate funds. Cost: near-zero rules, medium model operations. Metrics: precision/recall, false-positive rate, review time, subgroup disparity. Privacy/security: strict purpose/access/retention, security review. Oversight: trained human decides adverse action. Fallback: manual controls and provider checks.
- [ ] **X7 Complaint pattern analysis.** Problem: surface recurring quality/delivery/policy issues. Data: consented support cases, normalized outcome codes, linked order events; launch availability: support data absent. Method: taxonomy rules first, optional NLP clustering/classification with human labels. Benefit: faster systemic issue detection. Limits: language/local idioms and small sample distort clusters. Cost: low/medium batch inference. Metrics: classification F1, actionable finding rate, missed severe cases. Privacy/security: redact PII; restrict raw text and retention. Oversight: support lead validates and routes findings. Fallback: tag-based reports.

## 3. Administrator Control Center

### Functional backlog

- [ ] Customer account management: search, limited support actions, account status, consent/privacy requests, and audited access.
- [ ] Merchant verification and review: application queue, evidence checklist, identity/business/stall/location decisions, needs-changes/reject/appeal states, expiry/reverification, and reviewer notes.
- [ ] Product listing moderation: reports, prohibited/restricted categories, duplicate candidates, authenticity/condition evidence, version history, and takedown/appeal.
- [ ] Order monitoring: master/suborder/line/shipment drilldown, timeline, stale-state alerts, and exception actions.
- [ ] Payment status monitoring: provider transaction lookup, webhook/reconciliation state, duplicate notifications, failed payments, and read-only by default.
- [ ] Refund and dispute management: evidence view, policy eligibility, reason codes, maker-checker approvals, provider status, deadlines, and appeals.
- [ ] Merchant payout oversight: reconciliation, holds/releases, change history, discrepancy resolution, and dual authorization for sensitive payout changes.
- [ ] Hub receiving and verification: inbound scan, quantity/quality checks, discrepancy evidence, acceptance/rejection, and handoff audit.
- [ ] Inventory and shipment monitoring: reserved/on-hand reconciliation, partial shipment states, carrier updates, failed-attempt workflow, and proof of delivery.
- [ ] Delivery exception handling: delay, failed delivery, reroute/retry/collection/return-to-hub options and customer notification controls.
- [ ] Customer support management: ticket queue, order-linked evidence, ownership, priority/SLA, escalation, resolution and customer appeal.
- [ ] Marketplace performance reports: orders, conversion, fees, quality, delivery, complaints, cancellations, refunds, and definitions/data freshness.
- [ ] Merchant visibility and concentration reports: impressions, rank/position, eligible exposure, category/area slices, and concentration trends.
- [ ] Fraud/risk alerts: explainable signals, investigation records, false-positive feedback, and review-only actions until approved.
- [ ] AI performance monitoring: model/rules version, quality/latency/cost, drift, subgroup performance, overrides, fallback rate, and kill switch.
- [ ] Immutable audit log for sensitive reads/actions: actor, role, target, before/after, reason, approval, timestamp, request/correlation ID.
- [ ] Staff roles and permissions with least privilege, multi-factor authentication, scoped access, session controls, and periodic access review.

### Permission separation and approval controls

- [ ] Define distinct roles for support agent, merchant reviewer, hub operator, dispatch coordinator, finance operator, risk investigator, administrator, and auditor; implement permission checks server-side for every API and record.
- [ ] Separate ordinary operational permissions from sensitive financial, security, and administrative permissions. Support/hub staff must not edit payout destinations, issue unrestricted refunds, grant roles, or approve their own verification decisions.
- [ ] Require step-up authentication and reason codes for sensitive actions; use maker-checker approval for configured refund/payout thresholds, role grants, account suspension, and irreversible data exports.
- [ ] Make consequential actions append-only/audited; prevent staff from deleting or editing the audit record they created. Provide audit search/export only to authorized audit/security roles.
- [ ] Add permission-matrix tests, cross-tenant access tests, approval separation tests, and audit-completeness tests before release.
